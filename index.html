Mentor Director, Mentor Playbook

Goal of talk: to demistify mentoring & encourage others to mentor

What is mentoring?

Mentoring is supporting someone in order to facilitate their journey to getting where they've self-directed is their destination - it is not telling someone what to do or having all the answers. What's important is that you're there to provide support and let them learn from your experience.

When does it happen?

1:1 relationship

Educational environment

Day-to-day interactions

Knowledge sharing is imparting your experience. Quickest path or support to persevere.

What do you need?

You need...

Time

Give more than you take

Give more than you take: You benefit from mentoring as well, but your mindset needs to be that of benefitting the person you're mentoring always

The Learning Process

Stages of Competence

A diagram of the four stages of competence, in order: unconscious incompetence, conscious incompetence, conscious competence, and unconscious competence.

ANYTHING you've ever learned or will learn will take you through these stages and all their frustrations

Conscious incompetence: Easy to get overwhelmed; Need resources & discipline

Conscious competence: Takes time & motivation; Need practice & support

Unconscious competence: Need continued practice (can slide backwards if skill is not used)

What'll make the difference in whether you give up or move to the next level is perseverance & support

This helps you develop empathy for yourself & others through the learning process

A mentor is someone who supports you through this process & helps keep you motivated so you don't give up

If you have a handful or half a handful of things that you've reached this level at in your field, you've familiarized yourself with the learning process and can help others through it

Stages of Competence

Jargon

Jargon is a shortcut to communication

Only helpful when there's a prior shared understanding

Vocab & jargon is 2nd nature, communication must become deliberate

Am I ready?

It's about two people in a developmental relationship who are supporting mutual learning and growth

One of my favorite things I've heard about mentoring is that no matter where you are, someone is working towards getting there.

Who to mentor?

Who asks for help

With potential

Dealing with change

With potential: Might be the quiet one who you notice has a good intuition or might be a born leader

Mentor someone...

Inside & outside of your network

Find someone outside of your network, who maybe even has a different background than you, so their varied perspective can help you identify and overcome your own unconscious biases

How should we mentor?

We tend to treat mentoring as if it's an innate skill, or an easy skill, and it's not - it's a skill like any other that needs to be learned, practiced, and improved with time

The essential...

Building trust

Confidentiality

Reliable

Share of yourself, don't hide

So that you can build credibility and effectively mentor

The essential...

Assume infinite intelligence, zero knowledge

When in doubt, blame the docs

Adjust your guidance to match their pace

The essential...

Colleague first, mentor second

Know-it-all approach is less effective, it can be intimidating

Put the relationship first

Don't know?

Hit Pause

'Hit Pause' when you don't have the right information, experience, or emotional state to provide a good answer to give you a chance to use your resources and come back with a clear and valuable response

Saying "I don't know" makes you relatable, it would be intimidating if you knew all the things

Make it clear that they're expected to follow through & be reliable

Also, detail what's the best way to reach you & when are you unavailable

Formulate good responses

Approachable

Vulnerable

Present

Approachable: Non-judgemental; do not shame anyone in their learning process

Vulnerable: Share your own failures & mishaps

Present: Shows that you're receptive and available

Body language is important; eye contact, open stance, active listening; 65% is non-verbal

Formulate good responses

Encouraging

Walk the middle path

Non-directive

Be especially encouraging to the first few questions

Walk the middle path: don't go too deep or too shallow in your explanations; Avoid the trap of too much specificity so you don't overwhelm them

Do not assume that you know what's best for them, what they need needs to be self-determined by them

Answers need to be non-directive to help them reach a conclusion

Formulate bad responses

  • ✗ Well, actually...
  • ✗ It's easy.
  • ✗ Just XYZ...

Well, actually: Often provides minor corrections not conducive to furthering understanding

It's easy: Could imply it's so easy, the learner shouldn't need help

Just XYZ...: Might solve the situation but is not transferrable

Formulate good responses

  • ✔ What have you already considered?
  • ✔ What went as planned?

Considered?

What needs to be changed?

Non-directive instruction

Validate their input as intelligent and valuable, jump-starts ideas and discussion

Formulate good responses

  • ✔ What do you need to feel supported?
  • ✔ When X, do Y, because Z...

When X, do Y, because Z...: Great for code reviews & other technical situations

Mentor better

Mentor better...

Self care

Lead by example to create a healthy relationship/community

Take breaks when needed - you can't pour from an empty cup

Know your limits, set & maintain boundaries

Mentor better...

Emotional intelligence

Made up of empathy, personal competence (self-awareness and self-management), social competence (social awareness, relationship management)

Signs that it is probably not a developmental moment: • Defensive body language • Expressions of high frustration • Distracted or impatient

Know when it's time to encourage them to take a break, or when they might need a push

Mentor better...

Cultural & personal sensitivities

What is their body language communicating

Keep a lookout for your own blindspots

Acknowledge & appreciate differences, in perspectives as well

They've had different experiences and WILL have different experiences even with the same actions

Outlive the relationship

Future benefits

Focus on character development

Rather than competency

Build them up so that they can make good decisions without you

Your goal is for them to rely on you less & less

Future benefits

Forever mentor

Long-term guidance, which will help them make decisions that outlive their time with you

Ask for feedback

Reiterate and improve based on that feedback

Why mentor?

Mentor because...

Develop skills

Reinforce knowledge

Expand your network

Develop skills: These include leadership & communication skills

Reinforce knowledge: Finding new ways to teach concepts to beginners

Expand your network: Get to know your fellow developers outside of your normal peer groups

Mentor because...

Build community

Make a positive impact on your community by helping create a culture of supportive growth

Mentor because...

Nurture belonging

We know intuitively that diversity matters, but the introduction of the varying perspectives that come with diversity is a marker in measuring tangible success, even financial success

If you're looking around for example and you don't see enough women or people of color, find someone in that group to support and mentor
